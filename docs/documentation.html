Overview

The whole idea in a minute

You type a username. The app asks Meta what that account has published, saves the metadata and thumbnails locally, and draws a grid. Nothing full-resolution is fetched yet — thumbnails are cheap, originals are not. You click the tiles you want, give them a base filename, and press Download. The originals arrive, get cached forever, and get copied into your output folder as base_01.jpg, base_02.mp4, and so on.

Two facts shape every design decision in the codebase, and both are worth knowing before you read another line.

Fact one: the API only sees business accounts

Data comes from Meta's Business Discovery API, which reads public Business and Creator accounts only. Personal accounts are invisible to it. That is a property of the API, not a gap in this app, and no workaround exists. Business Discovery also has no per-media endpoint: a media URL exists only inside a listing response, and those URLs expire — which is why re-listing a profile is a first-class recovery path rather than an edge case.

Fact two: exactly one thread talks to Instagram

The API process runs uvicorn with --workers 1: an HTTP event loop plus exactly one background fetch thread. That thread is the only code in the system permitted to make an Instagram-bound request. Serialization is therefore structural — not a convention someone has to remember. Everything else — the rate pacer, the circuit breaker, the job queue, the local reel budget — hangs off that single choke point.

Blast radius. All Meta Graph usage lives in api/app/ig/. When Instagram reshapes a response, exactly one file needs changing: api/app/ig/graph_map.py. A live canary test exists to tell you when that day has arrived.

Scope & non-goals

What this is, and what it deliberately is not

In scope

  • Public Business and Creator accounts
  • Reading metadata, thumbnails and originals
  • Individual downloads at maximum available quality
  • Reels with their audio intact
  • A local record of tracked profiles and of every action taken
  • One machine, one user, one credential

Out of scope

  • Private accounts, and personal accounts of any kind
  • Publishing, scheduling, or any write access to Instagram
  • Unattended bulk-archiving of an account's full history
  • Per-user Instagram logins or a multi-tenant deployment
  • ZIP bundles — files are written individually, by design
  • Stories, highlights, comments, DMs
Known limits, stated plainly. View counts exist on reels and nowhere else, so reels weigh more heavily in any engagement total. The trending day window is only as full as your cache — if nothing published in the last 24 hours has been synced, the feed is empty and says so. And the reel video path is the one place the app touches instagram.com directly; it is capped at 20/hour and 100/day on purpose.

Walkthrough

Lookup to download, click by click

The main flow. Everything below happens in one browser tab — keep it open while a download runs, because the browser drives the loop.

  1. Type a username on the home screen

    One input, one button. Recently tracked profiles sit underneath as one-click suggestions. Pressing Enter fires POST /api/profiles/{u}/sync, writes a lookup row into the activity log, wakes the worker, and routes you to /p/{username}.

  2. The profile page opens before the data exists

    You land on a loading state, not an error. The page polls until the sync lands, then swaps in the header: avatar, full name, biography, follower and post counts, last sync time, and how many posts are already cached locally.

    A private account gets its own explicit state — the app tells you the account is out of reach rather than showing an empty grid.

  3. Behind the scenes, the worker walks the profile

    profile_sync fetches metadata and the avatar, then enqueues page_fetch. The first page is small (12 posts — enough to fill one screen) and every later page is 36. Each post's metadata, engagement counts, source URLs and thumbnail land in SQLite and on disk.

  4. Browse the grid

    Tiles are newest-first. Switch tabs — Posts Reels Saved Recreate — search captions, or flip on cached only. A match count and a reset button appear whenever a filter is active, so you always know why a tile is missing.

  5. Open a tile to look closer, without spending a download

    The lightbox streams the original from Meta's CDN through /api/media/{shortcode}/preview, one slide at a time for a carousel. This is a 307 redirect on purpose: an expired URL fails as a broken image you can fall back from, never as a cached wrong answer.

  6. Select what you want

    Click to toggle, shift-click to take a range, or select every visible tile. The selection bar counts what you picked and lets you name and save the set for later.

  7. Name the batch and press Download

    The browser calls POST /api/media/ensure first, which queues whatever is not yet cached and reports a per-shortcode status. It polls until everything reads ready, showing progress as it goes, then calls POST /api/downloads.

  8. Files land in your output folder

    Ordered oldest-first, so the numbering reads chronologically — the opposite of the grid, deliberately. Singles become base_01.jpg; carousel slides become base_07a.jpg, base_07b.jpg. A name collision gets a -1 suffix rather than an overwrite. The download is recorded in the activity log with its file count and destination.

Close the tab mid-run? Nothing is lost. Media already fetched stays cached forever, so re-selecting the same posts finishes immediately.

Trending → recreate queue

  1. Open Trending

    The feed ranks every hashtag in your cached captions. Nothing here touches Instagram: it is a read-only view over what a sync already brought in.

  2. Pick a window

    Day covers the last 24 hours, Week the last 7 days. Switching re-queries rather than re-filtering, so the score is always computed over exactly the window shown.

  3. Read the score honestly

    A tag's score is the likes, comments and views of every cached post carrying it, added together — so a tag several decent posts share outranks one that rode a single hit. Ties break on post count, then alphabetically, which keeps offset paging stable.

  4. Expand a row

    The posts behind that tag load as thumbnails, newest first. Ones already on the recreate queue are labelled queued. Pick any of them and send the batch to the queue; the local selection is scratch and is thrown away when the row closes.

Recreate → prepare

  1. The queue lists what you marked

    Split by status: selected and prepared. Each row is a snapshot taken at the moment you marked it — username, permalink and caption — so clearing a profile's cache can never quietly delete queued work.

  2. Open one to choose slides

    A single post has no choice to make. A carousel shows a slide picker; the default is all of it. Downloaded slides render from disk, the rest from the CDN preview.

  3. Press Submit to build the payload

    Every path in the result is read out of media_files, so a payload can never name a file that was never downloaded. A post that is not downloaded yet gets a 409 telling you to fetch it first.

  4. Prepared, not submitted

    The payload is shown in full, and the page says outright that nothing has been handed to a generator — because no generator exists yet. The word "submitted" is reserved for the day a handoff actually happens.

The header, always on

Every page carries the same shell: search, navigation, tracked profiles, the recreate queue badge, a theme switch, and a live status strip polling /api/status every five seconds. It shows the worker state and queue depth, Meta's reported quota percentage, your remaining reel budget with a countdown to reset, and — when something has gone wrong — one of three banners.

BannerMeansWhat to do
Rate limited The worker paused itself after a 429, or because Meta's usage counter crossed your ceiling. Nothing. It resumes on its own, and the banner counts down.
Token invalid The token expired or was revoked. The breaker tripped permanently — it will not retry past a challenge on its own. Issue a new token, update .env, restart, then press I have fixed it.

Architecture

Two processes, one thread that matters

web is Next.js 15 with the App Router; it serves the UI and rewrites /api/* to api, so the browser only ever sees one origin. api is FastAPI on uvicorn with a single worker process. Inside it: the HTTP event loop, a SQLite database in WAL mode, and one daemon thread named ig-worker.

Request lifecycle

Two shapes of request, and the distinction matters.

Read answered immediately

Grid, trending, activity, status, thumbnails. The endpoint opens its own SQLite connection, reads, closes. Nothing queues, nothing waits on Instagram, and the response is entirely from local state.

Fetch answered with a promise

Sync, load-more, ensure. The endpoint writes a job row, wakes the worker, and returns straight away. The browser polls to watch state change. No HTTP request ever blocks on a network call to Meta.

The one worker

The loop is small enough to state completely: check the breaker; claim the highest-priority queued job in a BEGIN IMMEDIATE transaction; run its handler; record the outcome as one line of JSON. If nothing is queued it sleeps on an event with a 5-second timeout, so an enqueue wakes it instantly and a missed wake costs five seconds, not a stall.

JobPriorityDoesThen
media_fetch10 Downloads the originals for one post; falls back to the reel path when Meta publishes no file. Marks the item ready, or failed with the reason.
profile_sync20 Fetches profile metadata and the avatar. Enqueues page_fetch unless a walk is already part-way through.
page_fetch30 Fetches one Graph page, upserts every post, caches missing thumbnails. Stores the next cursor — or an empty string, which means exhausted forever.

Lower number wins. A download you asked for is always served before background indexing, which is the ordering a person waiting at a screen would choose.

Three independent brakes

BrakeEnforcesDefault
Graph pacerFloor on the gap between Graph API calls.3s

The circuit breaker

One global gate, with two very different modes. A temporary trip is a timed nap, taken after a rate limit, and expires on its own — the wait is max(IG_BACKOFF, whatever Meta estimated), because coming back sooner than asked is how a rate limit becomes a blocked IP. A permanent trip follows a login challenge or a dead token and never expires by itself; automatically retrying past a challenge is how a throttled account becomes a flagged one. Clearing it is a deliberate human act: POST /api/health/clear-breaker, which is what the banner button calls.

The local reel budget

Business Discovery publishes a reel's thumbnail and keeps the video, so reel video is the one thing fetched from instagram.com directly, through yt-dlp. That path has its own ledger in quota_events: 20 per hour, 100 per day, a 20-second floor between calls and a 120-second timeout. Because we own the ledger, the reset time is a fact rather than an estimate — which is why the header can show a real countdown here and cannot for Meta's quota. Checking the cap and recording the attempt happen in one indivisible step, so two callers cannot both see the last slot as free. There is deliberately no user-agent rotation and no randomised "human" sleeps: Instagram fingerprints request patterns, not headers, so disguise buys exposure without protection. The honest lever is doing less.

Failure taxonomy

Every adapter failure is mapped onto one of six cases before it reaches the worker. The mapping is the whole point: a rate limit, a dead post and a network blip call for opposite responses, and one generic error would make them look alike.

Secrets never travel. The token goes in an Authorization header, never a query string, because a URL ends up in exception text, the JSON logs and the activity table. Every error message is passed through a redactor before it is stored or raised.

Adapters and the runtime switch

One interface, IgAdapter: fetch_profile, fetch_page, fetch_media, download_to, login. Two implementations satisfy it — graph, which talks to Meta, and fake, which serves a deterministic in-memory dataset with no network at all. A contract test runs the same assertions against both, so the fake cannot quietly drift from the real one.

Switching adapters at runtime swaps a single frozen triple — name, settings, adapter — in one assignment, so there is no window where the adapter is already fake while the paths still address the real database. The fake adapter's database and media cache are nested under a fake/ directory, so isolation is enforced by the filesystem rather than by every query remembering to filter. Your OUT_DIR is deliberately untouched: it holds files you named. Everything that can fail happens before the swap, and the choice is persisted last — to adapter.state beside the database, not a row inside it, since you cannot know which database to open until you have read the selection.

Every write is atomic: bytes go to name.part, get fsync'd, then os.replace onto the final path. A process killed mid-fetch leaves a .part file and no half-valid cache entry, and the boot sweep deletes it.

Data model

Seven tables, one SQLite file

WAL journal, foreign keys on, a 5-second busy timeout. Requests get a fresh connection each; the worker keeps a long-lived one cached per thread and keyed by path, so an adapter switch cannot leave it writing to the database it started with. Schema changes that add columns run through an idempotent migration, because SQLite has no ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS.

TableHoldsNotable columns
profiles One row per account ever looked up. next_cursor — NULL means nothing indexed yet, "" means exhausted forever; tracked, avatar_path, last_synced_at
media_items One row per post, keyed by shortcode. kind (image · video · reel · carousel), taken_at, caption, like_count, comments_count, view_count, full_status, src_urls
media_files One row per file on disk, unique on (shortcode, role, child). role = thumb | full, child_idx, path, mime, bytes, width, height
jobs The work queue itself. kind, payload (JSON, sorted keys — which is what makes deduplication work), priority, status, attempts
activity_log Every action, newest first. action = lookup · load_more · track · untrack · download, target, detail (JSON)
quota_events Our own reel ledger. Rows older than 7 days are pruned on write. kind, at
recreate_items Posts lined up to be remade. A snapshot, not a view. status = selected | prepared, slides, payload, prepared_at

The status a tile shows

media_items.full_status is the single source of truth behind every tile badge, every progress bar and every retry button. 

none ──ensure──▶ queued ──worker claims──▶ fetching ──▶ ready

How trending is computed

Nothing is stored. The window in front of every query bounds the scan to recent posts, and the functions are pure enough that a materialised index could be slid underneath them if that ever stops being cheap.

  • A # counts at the start of a caption or after whitespace, and nowhere else — otherwise example.com/#anchor becomes a trending tag.
  • The tag body is taken by Unicode category (letters, numbers, marks, connector punctuation), not by \w. \w excludes combining marks, which cuts a Devanagari tag at its first vowel sign and quietly makes the whole feature Latin-only.
  • Tags are casefolded, not lowercased, so Greek ΚΌΣΜΟΣ, κόσμος and κόσμοσ are one tag rather than three.
  • Tags are deduplicated per post: a caption repeating #sale three times should not outrank a post people actually engaged with.
  • score = likes + comments + views, summed over every cached post in the window, nulls counted as zero.
  • Sorted by score, then post count, then tag — three keys, because offset paging with a partial order makes pages repeat or drop rows.

How the fixtures are built

The backend suite gets a Settings pointed at tmp_path, a schema'd SQLite connection, a FakeAdapter pre-loaded with the nike profile and ten posts, and a TestClient wired to all three with the worker thread switched off — so tests drive handlers directly and assert on exact state rather than racing a background thread.

The e2e database is seeded by running the real handlers against the fake adapter (api/scripts/seed_e2e.py), not by hand-written SQL. Seeded state is therefore produced by production code paths, and a handler bug shows up in the seed rather than hiding behind fixtures that agree with it.

Worked examples

1 — A pure unit: does the ranking rank?

No database, no HTTP. Just rows in, order out.

# api/tests/test_hashtags.py
def test_a_tag_is_scored_by_the_engagement_of_its_posts():
    rows = [
        {"caption": "#nike #run", "like_count": 100, "comments_count": 5, "view_count": None},
        {"caption": "#run",       "like_count": 10,  "comments_count": 0, "view_count": None},
    ]

    ranked = hashtags.rank(rows)

    assert [r["tag"] for r in ranked] == ["run", "nike"]   # 115 beats 105
    assert ranked[0]["post_count"] == 2

2 — An HTTP test: does the window actually bound the query?

Rows are inserted directly here rather than driven through the fake adapter, because these tests are about the window and the ranking — hand-written rows say exactly what date and engagement each post has. 

# api/tests/test_api_hashtags.py
def test_trending_ranks_tags_inside_the_window(client, conn):
    _insert(conn, "a", "#nike #run", hours_ago=2, likes=100, comments=5)
    _insert(conn, "b", "#run",       hours_ago=3, likes=10)

    body = client.get("/api/hashtags/trending?window=day").json()

    assert body["window"] == "day"
    assert [i["tag"] for i in body["items"]] == ["run", "nike"]
    assert body["items"][0]["score"] == 115

3 — A failure path: does a spent cap stay out of the breaker?

The interesting assertion is the negative one. Our own budget running out must not make the app back off from Meta, which is a completely separate quota. 

# api/tests/test_worker_runner.py
def test_a_spent_local_cap_fails_the_job_without_tripping_the_breaker(...):
    # handler raises IgQuotaSpent
    worker._run_job(conn, current, job)

Writing a new case

  • Name the behaviour, not the function. Every test in this repo reads as a sentence — test_a_spent_local_cap_fails_the_job_without_tripping_the_breaker — so a failure line tells you what broke without opening the file.
  • Pick the cheapest level that can see the bug. Ranking logic belongs in a pure unit; the window boundary belongs at the HTTP layer; a state race belongs in Playwright.
  • Avoid position-based mocks. A fixture that says "the third seeded item" breaks the day the seed order changes. Assert on the shortcode.
  • Leave shared server state as you found it. The recreate queue and the adapter selection persist across specs; clean up in afterEach.
  • Run the canary when Instagram gets weird. A canary failure means graph_map.py needs updating — re-record fixtures first with python scripts/capture_graph_fixtures.py nike.

Getting the Graph credentials

Business Discovery reads other accounts through an account of your own, so there is a one-time setup. None of it can be automated.

  1. Convert an Instagram account to Business or Creator.
  2. Create or choose a Facebook Page and link the Instagram account to it. Business Discovery is only available through the Facebook-Login flavour of the API, so this link is mandatory.
  3. Create a Meta developer app and add the Instagram product.
  4. Grant the token instagram_basic, pages_show_list, pages_read_engagement and business_management.
  5. Find your Instagram user id: GET /me/accounts for the Page id, then GET /{page-id}?fields=instagram_business_account.
  6. Prefer a Business Manager system-user token, which does not expire, over a long-lived user token, which expires after 60 days.

Then verify without starting the stack:

cd api && IG_GRAPH_TOKEN=... python -m pytest tests/test_live_canary.py -v

--workers 1 is not a tuning knob. A second uvicorn worker is a second fetch thread, and the whole rate-limit story collapses: the pacer, the breaker and the reel ledger all assume exactly one caller.

Troubleshooting

When something is wrong

SymptomLikely causeFix
"has not been synced" on a profile you just looked up The sync job failed. The 404 body carries last_error from the failed job row. Read that message — it is usually "not a Business account" or a token problem.
Profile exists on Instagram but the app says it is private It is a personal account. Business Discovery cannot see it. Nothing to fix. This is the API's boundary, not a bug.
Empty grids and odd errors across the board Instagram probably rotated something in the response shape. Run the canary. A failure means api/app/ig/graph_map.py needs updating — re-record fixtures first.
Downloads sit at queued forever Worker stopped, or the breaker is tripped. Check /api/status: worker and breaker tell you which.
A reel refuses to download with a quota status Your own hourly or daily reel cap is spent. This is refused at the click rather than queued against a spent budget. Wait for the countdown in the header, or raise the cap in .env.
"too far back to refresh; re-sync to open it" The cached CDN URL expired and the post is deeper than IG_GRAPH_REFRESH_PAGES. Re-sync the profile, or raise that setting.
Meta returns nothing but the token looks fine Usage guard: the peak x-app-usage reading crossed your ceiling and the adapter is refusing to spend calls. Wait for Meta's hourly window to reset, or raise IG_GRAPH_USAGE_LIMIT.
Another local project died when you ran the tests e2e-native.sh force-kills ports 3000 and 8000. Stop that service before running the suite.

Reading the logs

Every job outcome is one line of JSON — job_id, kind, target, duration_ms, outcome — so grep and jq both work on it without a parser. Outcomes are done, rate_limited, challenge, quota_spent, terminal, transient, or crashed. The boot sweep logs its own line saying how many .part files, items and jobs it recovered.
